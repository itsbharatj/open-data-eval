# VLN-CE (R2R-CE) state of the art 2023-2026, transfer to HA-VLN 2.0 (HA-R2R-CE), and realistic SR ceilings

Topic id: `B2-vlnce-sota-2023-2026` (second pass, 2026-10-24; supersedes the first-pass draft written at 19:02 UTC)

## Summary

R2R-CE val-unseen SR rose from 57-61 (ETPNav 57, BEVBert 59, HNR 61, g3D-LF 61; Habitat 0.1.7, CWP depth waypoint predictor) to 65-68 for RL-tuned graph agents (ETP-R1 65.3, Topo-RFT 68.1), and to 69-77 for 2026 native-MLLM policies (OmniNav 69.5, Qwen-RobotNav 72.1, SimpleVLN-Qwen3.5-9B 76.7). These SRs do NOT transfer to the challenge, because the challenge SR is success with zero collisions (walls included; the per-episode collision_num allowance is 0 in >90% of episodes). The decisive new evidence comes from re-analysing the released per-episode logs of 'Successful but Unsafe' (vln-safe, MIT). That study ran six public agents in Habitat 0.1.7 with exactly the HA-VLN physics: radius 0.1 m, 0.25 m / 15 deg, sliding, and the same previous_step_collided predicate. Their zero-collision success on human-free R2R-CE val_unseen is only: ETP-R1 37.7 (raw SR 64.6), StreamVLN 34.4, ETPNav 34.0, OmniNav 29.1 (raw 67.5), CMA 17.8. Collision handling moves these numbers: a training-free depth guard lifts ETP-R1 to 50.1, and a navmesh-oracle guard (privileged, legal in offline replay) lifts it to 61.3 at a cost of only ~2 raw-SR points. In ensembles, at least one of ETP-R1/StreamVLN/OmniNav stops within 3 m on 87.2% of episodes (91.5% with six agents), so public agents are excellent goal proposers. On HA-VLN-CE itself, the best verified published numbers are small. HCSG (May 2026, code not released) reaches val-unseen SR 0.24 / CR 0.36, against BEVBert 0.21 and ETPNav 0.17. In challenge Score units that is ~16-23, against the CMA baseline's 12.9. DyGeoVLN (Mar 2026) claims SR 0.40 (Score ~34), but its collision counts are inconsistent with the all-collision metric. Minimal-port candidates for Habitat 0.1.7 are ETP-R1, ETPNav, BEVBert, g3D-LF, OmniNav, NaVid/Uni-NaVid and NaVILA (0.1.7-native), plus StreamVLN (already run on 0.1.7 by vln-safe through a habitat-free worker). The vln-safe harness itself is the fastest integration backbone. ETPNav-family official eval uses back_algo=control, so its rollouts ARE replayable primitive tapes (this corrects the first draft). Its 80-token instruction cap truncates the ~140-token HA-R2R instructions, though. Realistic ceilings on HA-R2R test by Nov 30: pure egocentric closed loop 0.25-0.40 SR (Score ~28-42); closed loop with HA fine-tuning and a navmesh-oracle collision layer 0.45-0.60 (Score ~45-60); ensemble goal proposal plus VLM verification plus a collision-free planner 0.70-0.87 (Score ~70-85); perfect goal knowledge 0.97-1.0 (Score ~95-98). Goal inference, not control, decides the leaderboard.

## Five most decision-relevant findings

1. **Zero-collision success is the metric, and public agents are bad at it.** On human-free R2R-CE, under HA-VLN-identical physics, ETP-R1 gets clean SR 37.7 against raw SR 64.6, and OmniNav 29.1 against 67.5. A navmesh-oracle guard lifts ETP-R1 to 61.3 clean at a cost of only ~2 raw-SR points. Privileged collision-free execution is cheap and mandatory.
2. **Public agents are strong goal proposers.** At least one of ETP-R1/StreamVLN/OmniNav stops within 3 m on 87.2% of val_unseen episodes (91.5% with six agents), versus 67.5% for the best single agent. The selector decides the score.
3. **The published HA-VLN-CE bar is low.** The best verified result is HCSG at SR 0.24 / CR 0.36, about Score 22; BEVBert is 0.21 and ETPNav 0.17, against the CMA baseline's 13.0. DyGeoVLN's SR 0.40 uses a non-comparable collision count.
4. **Minimal-port path exists.** vln-safe (MIT) already runs ETP-R1, ETPNav, BEVBert, g3D-LF, StreamVLN, NaVILA and OmniNav in Habitat 0.1.7, with the guard included. ETPNav-family official eval uses back_algo=control, so its rollouts are replayable primitive tapes. Fix the 80-token instruction cap (HA-R2R is about 140 tokens).
5. **Ceilings.** Egocentric closed loop reaches 0.25-0.40 SR. Closed loop with fine-tuning and an oracle collision layer reaches 0.45-0.60. Ensemble goal proposal, a verifier and a planner reach 0.70-0.87. Perfect goals reach 0.97-1.0 (Score ~95-98).

## Corrections vs. the first-pass draft and vs. CONTEXT.md

- The first-pass draft said ETPNav's default eval teleports when backtracking. **Wrong for published numbers**: eval/inference scripts set `IL.back_algo control`, so movement is executed with primitives. `teleport` is only the yaml/training default.
- The first-pass draft said OmniNav had no code. **Wrong**: github.com/amap-cvlab/OmniNav has training and inference code, ModelScope weights, and runs on Habitat 0.1.7.
- The first-pass draft placed g3D-LF on Habitat 0.1.7. Its environment.yml pins habitat-sim 0.2.1, although its README defers to ETPNav's 0.1.7 install.
- The HA-VLN-CMA val-unseen row differs between HA-VLN 2.0 versions (63.84/0.75/0.04 vs 47.06/0.77/0.07). Both are worse than the challenge CMA* (17.5/0.684/0.129).
- No contradiction with CONTEXT.md facts 1-8. Facts 3 and 8 are strengthened: all-collision counting is consistent with HA-VLN 2.0's TCR magnitudes, and privileged collision-free execution is shown to be nearly free in SR.

## Implications (inferences)

- The challenge metric is zero-collision success, with walls counted. Even on human-free R2R-CE, no public agent exceeds 38% clean SR unguarded, so published R2R-CE SR (57-77) overstates challenge SR by roughly 2x. Collision handling alone is worth +12 to +24 SR points before any language improvement (ETP-R1: 37.7 -> 50.1 with the depth guard, -> 61.3 with the navmesh oracle).
- Because scoring is replay of an action tape, local execution may use privileged geometry: the navmesh with humans inserted as static objects, exactly as HA-VLN builds it. vln-safe's oracle guard shows that privileged collision vetoing costs a learned policy only ~2 raw-SR points. Combined with the HA-VLN navmesh (humans included), it also removes human collisions. Every closed-loop agent should run behind a navmesh-oracle collision layer and be verified by local replay.
- Observation rigs are irrelevant to scoring. ETPNav's 12-view RGB-D panorama, StreamVLN's 640x480 HFOV 79, OmniNav's three 720x640 HFOV 110 cameras at 1.25 m, NaVILA's 512x512 and SimpleVLN's 640x480 HFOV 90 can all be rendered locally. Only the dynamics must match: 0.25 m / 15 deg, radius 0.1 m, height 1.5 m, sliding, the 0.1.7 navmesh with humans. 'Minimal porting' therefore means 'runs against the HA-VLN 0.1.7 simulator (or an exact geometric mirror) and emits primitives'.
- Minimal-port ranking for Habitat 0.1.7 + HA-VLN. (1) ETP-R1: 0.1.7-native, best graph agent, 4.5 s/episode, ckpts on HF, eval already uses back_algo control. It needs max_text_len raised from 150 and an HA scene/human loader. (2) OmniNav: 0.1.7-native, 67.5-69.5 raw SR, 3B model; waypoint teleports must be quantized to primitives, as vln-safe already does. (3) StreamVLN, NaVILA, NaVid/Uni-NaVid via vln-safe's habitat-free workers. (4) SimpleVLN: strongest open weights (76.7), but its eval is Habitat 0.3.3. The thin vLLM client is easy to call from a 0.1.7 harness; the cost is re-implementing its prompt and history sampling, and verifying parity on R2R-CE val_unseen.
- Language-side risk: HA-R2R instructions (~140 tokens) exceed ETPNav's 80-token cap, so the goal-describing tail is dropped. HA-VLN 2.0's low retrained ETPNav SR (0.17) may partly reflect this. For any R2R-trained policy, either raise the cap and fine-tune on HA-R2R train (10,819 episodes), or LLM-condense each instruction into R2R style, keeping the route and the final landmark, before feeding it.
- Ensembles are excellent goal proposers. On R2R-CE, the union of three public agents' stop points contains a correct (<=3 m) goal for 87% of episodes, while a naive '>=2 agree' consensus covers 67%. Goal-selection quality, via a VLM verifier over privileged renders, instruction-landmark matching and consensus clustering, sets the realized SR between ~0.67 and ~0.87, before HA-R2R language-shift losses. This dominates any improvement to closed-loop control.
- Competitive landscape in challenge Score units (val unseen). Organizers' CMA 13.0; HA-VLN 2.0 retrained ETPNav ~16, BEVBert ~19; HCSG (best verified, code unreleased) ~22; DyGeoVLN claims ~34 on a laxer metric. A team plugging a public agent into a guard will land around 30-45. A privileged offline pipeline with good goal inference can reach 70-85, and the theoretical ceiling with perfect goals is ~95-98. Expect the winning margin to come from goal inference accuracy and from deterministic collision-free tapes.
- Fine-tuning evidence. SimpleVLN shows that RL with a binary success reward adds 7.5-10 SR on R2R-CE. For the challenge, the reward should be success AND zero collisions on the human-inserted navmesh, with expert trajectories re-planned on an inflated navmesh. HCSG's ablations show that social-distance losses add ~3 SR and cut CR by 0.15 on HA-VLN-CE, and that depth is essential: RGB-only loses 8 SR points.
- Nondeterministic human frames during official replay (CONTEXT fact 4) are second-order for closed-loop agents. Plan and verify on the union (swept volume) of all 120 frames' human footprints, so the tape is collision-free regardless of which frame the scorer is on.

## Recommendations

- Adopt vln-safe (MIT; pinned repos, checkpoint sha256s, habitat-free VLA workers, guard and oracle-guard code, per-episode logging) as the closed-loop integration backbone. Swap its simulator setup for HA-VLN's HASimulator (human GLBs as static objects + navmesh recompute; swept-volume variant for planning). Add a primitive action-tape exporter matching track2/scripts/export_cma_submission.py.
- Bring up ETP-R1 first: 0.1.7-native, ~4.3 GPU-h per full test pass. Set IL.back_algo control, raise max_text_len to >=256 (or condense instructions), wrap it in the oracle guard, and measure challenge-metric SR on HA-R2R val_unseen with metric.py. Then fine-tune on HA-R2R train (DAgger + GRPO with reward = success AND TCR==0).
- Bring up OmniNav-3B (0.1.7-native, highest open raw SR among 0.1.7 agents) and SimpleVLN-Qwen3.5-9B (vLLM). Use them with ETP-R1 as a 3-way goal-proposer ensemble: on R2R-CE the union covers 87% of goals. Log every stop point plus OSR waypoints as candidates for the goal-inference pipeline (C8/B4/B10).
- Never submit a raw policy rollout. Re-execute the chosen goal with the collision-free planner on the inflated, human-inserted navmesh, verify zero collisions by local replay in the organizer docker image, and minimize NE by stopping at the candidate goal centre.
- Use the HCSG/HA-VLN 2.0 numbers (SR 0.17-0.24, Score ~16-23) as the 'published SOTA' bar in the technical report. Treat DyGeoVLN's 0.40 as non-comparable unless re-measured with metric.py.
- For the Best-Innovative-Solution narrative, frame the system as 'ensemble VLN goal proposal + VLM verification + certified collision-free execution'. Cite vln-safe's finding that 38-57% of 'successful' VLN-CE episodes collide, which motivates certified execution.
- Measure on val_unseen in this order: (a) oracle planner with GT goal (target SR >= 0.97); (b) single-agent goal + planner; (c) ensemble-union oracle (upper bound for the selector); (d) ensemble + selector. The gaps between (b), (c) and (d) tell where to spend compute.
- If time permits, run SimpleVLN-style pass@k sampling (temperature 0.3, K=4-16) per test episode to enlarge the candidate goal set. Selection must not use ground truth on test, so it is a verifier problem (B10).

- Whether the 18 test scans' human placements will ship with the Phase 2 bundle. Without them, a navmesh-oracle collision layer must assume worst-case human occupancy, using human_motion_text.json priors from HA-VLN 1.0.
- HCSG code release timing, and whether HCSG or DyGeoVLN authors are Track 2 participants.
